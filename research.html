Research

Who bears water risk, measured where people live

My work asks how water risk is distributed in climate-stressed South Asia, and how to measure it locally rather than as averages. My thesis did this for drinking water in one coastal upazila. My current paper does it for water stress from a new industrial demand.

Research interests

My research interests lie in water quality, water treatment and sustainable water management, with freshwater scarcity as the thread that runs through them. I grew up in the coastal belt of southwestern Bangladesh, where groundwater is saline, surface water is unusable, and households store rainwater to drink through the dry season. As freshwater sources disappear, I want to work on alternative water sources, water reuse, treatment, and the processes that decide whether people have safe water.

I am interested in how environmental conditions, infrastructure and human activity affect water quality and the water available to people; how well treatment and management strategies actually work; and how data-driven and modelling approaches can help us understand and predict these systems. Two newer pressures sharpen this for me: climate change, which brings salinity, cyclones and floods to the coast, and the rapid growth of AI, whose data centres add water demand in regions that are already short of it.

Methods I have used: dry-season field sampling of household tanks; pH, turbidity, and total and fecal coliform analysis by membrane filtration at the BUET Environmental Engineering Laboratory; a 100-household survey; group comparisons with Holm correction for multiple tests; Python for data analysis.

Current work

Stress-Adjusted Water Impact of AI Inference Sites in Bangladesh and India

Working paper · In preparation

Estimates of AI’s water footprint usually report litres. But a litre drawn from a water-scarce basin costs a community more than a litre from a water-rich one. This paper applies a stress-adjusted water impact measure (Adjusted Water Impact, from the SCARF water-stress framework) to the same large-language-model inference workload placed at hypothetical data-centre sites in Bangladesh and India. The aim is to show how much siting changes the real water burden of identical AI use, and what that means for planning in the Global South.

Thesis · 2025

Variation of Rainwater Quality During Storage in Non-Rainy Seasons: A Case Study in a Coastal Area of Bangladesh

Undergraduate thesis, BUET · Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Md. Abdul Jalil

Ten household tanks in Koyra, Khulna, tracked for 122 days. Coliform-free at the start; above the WHO guideline from October in both brick and plastic tanks. Brick tanks were consistently worse.

Future directions

My undergraduate thesis was a small descriptive study, and it showed me the limits of observation alone. In graduate research I want to use controlled experiments, field studies and quantitative methods to move from describing problems to designing and testing solutions. These are the directions I want to pursue:

Treatment and reuse

Making treatment and reuse work

How do water chemistry, contaminant properties and treatment conditions shape treatment performance, such as membrane fouling and contaminant removal? I want to help treatment systems remove more, use less energy, and make safe wastewater reuse practical.

Safe drinking water

From the source to the point of use

Where contamination of stored drinking water comes from, how well low-cost point-of-use treatment such as chlorination, filtration and solar disinfection works, and what the remaining contamination means for health, assessed through quantitative microbial risk assessment (QMRA).

Water security

Water stress under climate change and AI

Measuring water stress where it is felt, from salinity and cyclones on the coast to the water demand of AI data centres, which my current paper assesses for sites in Bangladesh and India. I want to use data-driven and modelling methods, including machine learning, to assess and predict water quality and stress.

Resource recovery

Waste as a resource

Recovering water, energy and materials from waste streams, from wastewater reuse to converting organic waste into fuels, as part of more sustainable and circular water and waste systems.

Skills I am building: experimental design, environmental modelling, statistical learning, and quantitative microbial risk assessment and environmental microbiology, through coursework alongside my current paper.

Long-term goal: to become an independent researcher who develops my own questions and tests them through careful experiments and field studies, and an educator who helps students use scientific evidence to solve environmental problems. Above all, I want to develop practical, evidence-based solutions that vulnerable communities can adapt to improve their water security.
