Variation of Rainwater Quality During Storage in Non-Rainy Seasons: A Case Study in a Coastal Area of Bangladesh

In salinity-affected Koyra, households depend on stored rainwater through the dry season. We followed ten household tanks for 122 days to see what happens to that water in storage.

Degree
BSc Civil Engineering (Environmental)
Department
Civil Engineering, BUET
Supervisor
Prof. Dr. Md. Abdul Jalil

Site
Koyra, Khulna
Fieldwork
26 Aug – Dec 2024

Study

We followed ten household tanks (five plastic, five brick) from the start of storage on 26 August 2024 through four sampling rounds, to 122 days. We measured pH, turbidity, and total and fecal coliform at the BUET Environmental Engineering Laboratory, and surveyed 100 households in neighbouring villages.

My role: I carried out the field sampling, laboratory analysis and statistical analysis.

Storage timeline

  1. Day 0 · 26 Aug

    Storage begins. No coliforms detected.

  2. October

    Both tank types exceed the WHO guideline of zero.

  3. November

    Total coliform peaks in both tank types.

  4. December · Day 122

    Plastic tanks: turbidity 0.5 NTU, coliform still 27.8 CFU/100 mL.

Key results

  • Contamination appeared in storage.No coliforms were detected at the start, but both tank types exceeded the WHO guideline of zero from October. Total coliform peaked in November at 67.2 ± 8.1 CFU/100 mL in brick tanks and 45.6 ± 5.9 in plastic tanks.
  • Tank material was associated with quality.Brick tanks had significantly higher turbidity and coliform counts than plastic tanks in every round from October (Holm-adjusted p < 0.05). Households and tanks differed in other ways too, so this is an association, not proof of cause.
  • Clear water was not safe water.In plastic tanks, turbidity fell to 0.5 NTU by December while mean total coliform was still 27.8 CFU/100 mL.
  • Households were aware of the risk.Only 35% of surveyed households considered their stored rainwater safe to drink, and 15% used no treatment.

Limitations and next question

This was a small descriptive study of one upazila and one season, analysed from recorded group means and standard deviations. It documents the problem but does not explain its mechanism. That is the question I want to pursue in graduate research.
